I wasn't only delivering flows and apps. I built a 111-tool MCP server that programmatically administers Power Platform - environments, solutions, flows, apps, connections, security roles - with three authentication modes, secure defaults, and audit logging. Then I did it again for UKG Dimensions.

Four-tier environment strategy. Managed solutions. Documented revert runbooks for production incidents. Backups taken before destructive changes. A threat model written for my own tooling. Secure-by-default flags so writes are OFF unless explicitly enabled. Dry-run previews on destructive operations.

1.5Handling The Three Hard Objections#

OBJECTION 1: "You have 3.5 years. This is a Senior Consultant role."

DO NOT: apologise, over-explain, or list years of adjacent experience. DO: reframe from time-served to scope-owned.

MODEL ANSWER:

"That's fair, and I'd rather answer it with scope than with years. For the last nine months I was the only Accenture person on a T-Mobile programme - everyone else on the team was a client employee. There was no delivery lead above me and no peer from my firm to hand things to. I shaped requirements directly with the Enterprise Badging Center administrator, designed the solution, built it across four environments, took it to production, wrote the runbooks including production revert procedures, and trained the client's new joiners on it. Alongside that I built tooling - a 111-tool MCP server that administers Power Platform programmatically, plus a second one for UKG. So the question I'd ask back is whether the role needs someone who has done the work end-to-end without cover. That is exactly what I have been doing."

OBJECTION 2: "You haven't done tenant-scale DLP administration."

DO NOT: claim it. They will find the edge in two follow-ups. DO: separate the concept (you know it) from the access (you didn't have it).

MODEL ANSWER:

"Correct - I haven't authored tenant DLP policy, and I want to be precise about why. At T-Mobile I was a maker inside a governed tenant, not a tenant admin. I've worked within DLP constraints, hit connector restrictions, and gone through the exception process. I understand the model - business / non-business / blocked classification, tenant versus environment scope, the precedence rules, and the fact that tightening a policy doesn't break running flows instantly, it fails them on next save or run, which is exactly why you do an impact assessment before you publish. I've since set up my own tenant and authored policies there to close the hands-on gap. What I bring is the governance instinct - I built secure-by-default gating into my own tooling, where write operations are hidden unless explicitly enabled."

OBJECTION 3: "Most of your work is one client, one domain."

MODEL ANSWER:

"One client, but a broad surface. In that programme I covered Canvas apps at Power Fx level, Dataverse modelling across six-plus custom tables, Power Automate at scale including SLA and expiry flows, a REST integration with CCure 9000 - a physical access control system - four-environment ALM with managed solutions, production incident response, and Python and Vue tooling around all of it. And I deliberately generalised: the MCP server pattern I built for Power Platform I rebuilt for UKG Dimensions, which is a completely different enterprise API. So I've proved the method transfers."

1.6What Not To Say (Guardrails)#

NEVER CLAIM:

X "I administered DLP policies at tenant scale" -> you did not X "I deployed the CoE Starter Kit in production" -> dev tenant only X "I ran an unattended RPA fleet" -> unless you truly did X "I'm PL-600 certified" -> you are not, yet X "I built dataflows for Dataverse ingestion" -> API-first instead X Any specific T-Mobile employee's name -> say "the EBC admin" X Real card numbers, employee IDs, or PII from the system

ALWAYS QUALIFY HONESTLY:

OK "I deployed CoE Kit in my own developer tenant to learn it properly" OK "I've built Copilot Studio agents at a working level, not enterprise scale" OK "I've done attended desktop flows; I haven't administered an unattended fleet" (adjust to the truth) OK "I'm actively preparing for PL-600, targeting <month>" OK "My integration work has been API-first rather than dataflow-based"

CONFIDENTIALITY - IMPORTANT

You worked on PHYSICAL SECURITY for a Fortune 100 telecom. Be careful. SAFE to discuss: architecture patterns, technology choices, general problem shapes, your role, how you solved things conceptually. NOT SAFE: real employee data, actual card numbers, facility lists, security clearance details tied to real people, internal URLs, credentials, specific vulnerabilities. If asked for detail you shouldn't give: "I'd rather not go into specifics since it's a physical security system, but the pattern was..." Interviewers RESPECT this. It signals you'll protect THEIR clients too. Mention that you wrote a THREAT_MODEL.md for your own tooling - it shows you think about this unprompted.

1.7The Accenture Internal Option (Do Not Skip)#

Before you apply externally, check Accenture's internal job board for a Power Platform Senior Consultant / Gurugram opening.

WHY:

  • Your delivery record is already visible internally.
  • Internal moves often skip several interview rounds.
  • You keep tenure, benefits, and any accrued leave.
  • Accenture has a large Power Platform practice in India.
  • You have a live reference: the T-Mobile EBC administrator.

WHY STILL APPLY EXTERNALLY:

  • An external offer is the strongest possible salary-negotiation lever,

internally or externally.

  • Internal promotions are usually capped at a fixed percentage band.
  • You said the goal is a meaningful pay increase. External offers move that

needle far more than internal band adjustments.

RECOMMENDED PLAY:

Do both, in this order.

  1. Apply externally now (the plan in PART 4).
  2. Simultaneously flag internal interest to your Accenture career counsellor.
  3. If the external offer lands, you have leverage either way.

Be aware: telling your counsellor you are looking carries some risk in some firms. Judge your own relationship there. If unsure, apply externally first and raise the internal option only once you have an offer in hand.
