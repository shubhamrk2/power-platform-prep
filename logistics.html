PART 08

Research & Logistics

Company research, the ERP angle, licensing, and how offers are lost.

Company research, application tactics, interview logistics, licensing, red flags, and the things that lose offers for reasons unrelated to skill

This part covers what Parts 1-7 did not: the non-technical machinery of actually getting hired, plus several technical topics that did not fit elsewhere but do come up.

8.1Researching The Employer (Do This Before Applying)#

You have not told me the company name - the JD says "Emerging ERP Solutions," which may be the practice/unit name rather than the firm. Find out which, then research accordingly.

WHAT TO FIND OUT (30-45 minutes, worth every minute)

  • Is it a Microsoft Partner? What competencies/designations do they hold?

(Solutions Partner for Business Applications is the relevant one) -> If yes, this directly explains why they care about certifications, and it strengthens the "will you fund PL-600" conversation.

  • What ERP do they actually do? Dynamics 365 F&O? Business Central? SAP?

Oracle? "Emerging ERP Solutions" suggests Dynamics 365, which would mean Power Platform is the extensibility layer around D365. THAT CHANGES YOUR EMPHASIS - see 8.2 below.

  • Client industries - do they serve regulated sectors? (Your badge/physical

security background is a differentiator if so.)

  • Headcount and Power Platform practice size - are you joining an

established practice or helping build one?

  • Recent news, funding, acquisitions, big client wins
  • Glassdoor / AmbitionBox reviews - especially on work-life balance and

whether "consultant" means travel

  • LinkedIn: find people with this exact title at the firm. What is their

background? Years of experience? That tells you the real bar far better than the JD does.

WHERE TO LOOK

Company website (careers + services pages), LinkedIn company page and employees, Microsoft Partner directory, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, Google News.

USE ONE SPECIFIC FACT IN THE INTERVIEW. "I noticed you're a Solutions Partner for Business Applications and most of your case studies are in [sector] - how much of the Power Platform work is D365 extension versus standalone?" That one question does more for you than any answer.

8.2The Erp Angle (Potentially Important, Currently Under-Covered)#

The role sits in an "Emerging ERP Solutions" practice. If that means Dynamics 365, your positioning shifts somewhat and you should prepare for it.

WHAT POWER PLATFORM MEANS IN A D365 CONTEXT

  • D365 CE (Sales, Customer Service, Field Service) is BUILT ON DATAVERSE.

Your Dataverse skills transfer directly and completely. Model-driven apps ARE the D365 CE UI. This is a big deal - say it.

  • D365 F&O (Finance & Operations) uses a different data store, surfaced to

Dataverse via DUAL-WRITE or virtual tables. Know these terms.

  • Power Platform is the standard extension mechanism: custom apps over D365

data, flows for process automation, custom connectors for third-party integration, Power BI for reporting.

  • Business Central similarly exposes data via connectors and APIs.

TERMS TO KNOW IF ASKED

DUAL-WRITE Near-real-time bidirectional sync between F&O and Dataverse. Table-mapping based. Notoriously fiddly to configure and to troubleshoot. VIRTUAL TABLES Dataverse tables backed by an external data source at read time - no data copy. Good when you must not duplicate data. DATA INTEGRATOR Older scheduled integration between F&O and Dataverse. FIRST-PARTY vs D365 apps are first-party solutions in your environment; CUSTOM your customisations layer on top. Same solution layering rules you already know.

YOUR HONEST POSITION

"I haven't worked on D365 F&O or Business Central directly. What I have is deep Dataverse - modelling, security, ALM, integration - which is the same foundation D365 CE runs on, and the extension patterns are the ones I've been using. The adjacent experience is UKG Dimensions, which is workforce management with Boomi as the integration layer, so I've worked around enterprise back-office systems and their integration middleware."

THAT LAST SENTENCE MATTERS. UKG and Boomi are exactly the vocabulary an ERP practice recognises. Lead with it in this context.

IF THE ROLE TURNS OUT TO BE HEAVILY D365 F&O

Be honest that it is a gap, and be honest with YOURSELF about whether you want it. A Power Platform role inside an F&O practice can mean a lot of dual-write troubleshooting, which is a specific and not-universally-loved skill.

8.3Licensing (Comes Up More Than People Expect)#

Consultants get asked this because it determines solution design and client cost. You do not need deep expertise, but blank ignorance looks bad.

WHAT DRIVES COST

  • STANDARD vs PREMIUM CONNECTORS. Premium (Dataverse, SQL, Salesforce, HTTP,

custom connectors) requires a premium licence for every user of the app or flow. This is the single most common licensing surprise.

  • Per-user vs per-app plans. Per-app licences a user for a limited number of

apps; per-user is unlimited.

  • Power Automate: per-user, per-user with attended RPA, and process

(previously per-flow) licensing for service-account-owned automation.

  • Unattended RPA is a separate paid add-on.
  • Dataverse capacity: database, file, and log storage, charged per GB above

the tenant entitlement.

  • AI Builder credits, Copilot Studio messages - consumption-based.
  • Power Apps/Automate included with some M365 licences ONLY FOR STANDARD

connectors within the M365 service boundary.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS TO MENTION

"Licensing shapes architecture more than people admit. If a flow uses a premium connector, everyone triggering it needs premium - so sometimes you restructure so a service-account-owned process flow does the premium work and users interact through something cheaper. Similarly, the choice between Power Automate and Logic Apps is often a licensing and governance decision rather than a technical one, since it's the same engine underneath."

DO NOT: quote specific prices. They change constantly and vary by region and agreement. Say "it depends on the agreement, but the model is..." and describe the structure.

8.4Topics Not Yet Covered (Technical Odds And Ends)#

These come up occasionally and are cheap to prepare.

POWER BI

Not in the must-haves, but adjacent and often assumed. Know: datasets vs reports vs dashboards, DirectQuery vs Import, row-level security, and that Power BI is the reporting layer over Dataverse (with the Dataverse connector or via Fabric/Synapse Link for analytics at scale). YOUR ANGLE: CoE Kit ships Power BI dashboards - if you set them up on Day 2, say so.

PCF (POWER APPS COMPONENT FRAMEWORK)

Custom code components (TypeScript/React) for model-driven and canvas apps, when out-of-box controls are not enough. Built and deployed with PAC CLI (pac pcf init / push). Worth knowing it EXISTS and that PAC CLI is how you work with it, even if you have not built one.

PLUGINS AND CUSTOM API

C# server-side extensibility registered in the Dataverse pipeline. Custom API lets you define your own Dataverse message callable from flows/apps. Know when you would reach for a plugin over a flow (see PART 5 Q19).

VIRTUAL TABLES / VIRTUAL CONNECTORS

Surface external data as Dataverse tables without copying it. Good answer to "how would you show data from an external system without syncing it?"

SYNAPSE LINK / FABRIC

Near-real-time export of Dataverse data to a data lake / Fabric for analytics without hammering the transactional store. Good answer to "how do you do reporting over millions of Dataverse rows?"

APPLICATION LIFECYCLE FOR CANVAS APPS SPECIFICALLY

  pac canvas unpack/pack for source control; acknowledge merge conflicts in
  canvas source are genuinely painful; component libraries for reuse.

ENVIRONMENT TYPES

Production, Sandbox, Trial, Developer, Teams (Dataverse for Teams), Default. KNOW THE DEFAULT ENVIRONMENT TRAP: every user in the tenant is automatically a maker in it, it cannot be deleted, and it is where ungoverned sprawl accumulates. First governance action on any client tenant: apply a restrictive DLP policy to the default environment. THAT IS A GREAT THING TO SAY UNPROMPTED in a governance conversation.

TENANT ISOLATION

Restricting which external tenants your users can connect to. Relevant to data exfiltration prevention alongside DLP.

MANAGED ENVIRONMENTS

Premium governance feature set: usage insights, sharing limits, data policies, solution checker enforcement, pipelines, IP firewall. Know it exists and what it adds - it comes up in governance discussions.

8.5Application Tactics#

HOW TO APPLY (in order of effectiveness)

  1. REFERRAL. By far the strongest. Check LinkedIn for anyone you know at the

firm, or anyone from your network who knows someone there. A referral gets your resume read by a human.

  1. Direct application on the company careers site.
  2. LinkedIn Easy Apply - lowest signal, highest volume. Use it only as a

backup.

  1. Message the hiring manager or a recruiter directly on LinkedIn with a short

note. Three sentences maximum: "Hi [name] - I saw the Senior Consultant Power Platform role in Gurugram. I've spent the last nine months as the sole Accenture resource embedded at T-Mobile owning a Power Platform programme end to end, including a REST integration into their physical access control system and ALM across four environments. I've applied through the portal - happy to share more if useful."

TIMING

Apply Monday-Wednesday morning if you can - application volume is lower and recruiters triage early in the week. This is a marginal effect, not a big one.

COVER LETTER / NOTE

Most Indian applications do not require one. If there is a free-text box, use it - four sentences addressing: why this role, your single strongest differentiator, the honest gap and what you are doing about it, and availability. Honesty about the gap in writing pre-empts the objection.

TRACK YOUR APPLICATIONS

Simple spreadsheet: company, role, date applied, source, contact, status, next action. You will apply to more than one role. Keep it organised.

APPLY TO MORE THAN THIS ONE ROLE

Genuinely important. Everything you build this week is reusable. Applying to five comparable roles rather than one does three things: increases your odds mechanically, gives you practice interviews before the one you care about, and creates competing-offer leverage. It also lowers the emotional stakes on any single outcome, which measurably improves how you present.

8.6Interview Logistics#

BEFORE

  • Confirm format: video or in-person, duration, who is interviewing, and

whether there is a technical exercise

  • If video: test camera, mic, and internet the day before, not 10 minutes

before. Use a wired connection if possible.

  • Neutral background, decent lighting from the front, camera at eye level
  • Close everything else on your machine - notifications during a screen

share are a bad look and can leak client information

  • Have on your desk: your numbers cheat sheet (PART 5 SECTION 5.10), your

questions for them, a pen and paper, water

  • IF SCREEN SHARING IS POSSIBLE: close every window with T-Mobile content.

Everything. Browser tabs, file explorer, VS Code. A single visible client filename is an unforced error.

DURING

  • Take a beat before answering. Silence feels longer to you than to them.
  • If you do not understand the question, ask. Answering the wrong question

confidently is worse than clarifying.

  • If you do not know, say so, then say what you would do to find out. "I

haven't hit that - my approach would be to check X and test Y" is a strong answer.

  • Signpost long answers: "There are three parts to that - the data model, the

integration, and the governance." Then deliver three parts.

  • Watch the interviewer. If they look bored, stop talking and ask if they want

more depth.

  • Take notes. It signals engagement and you will need them for the thank-you

note.

THE TECHNICAL EXERCISE (if there is one) Could be: a live design/whiteboard, a take-home build, or screen-shared building in a maker portal.

  • THINK OUT LOUD. They are assessing reasoning, not just the answer.
  • CLARIFY REQUIREMENTS FIRST. Jumping straight to a solution is the most

common failure. See PART 5 Q68 for the structure.

  • State assumptions explicitly.
  • If you get stuck, narrate it: "I'd normally check the documentation for the

exact syntax here - conceptually what I'm doing is..."

AFTER

  • Send a short thank-you within 24 hours. Three or four sentences.

Reference one specific thing from the conversation. Do not use a template that sounds like one.

  • Write down every question you were asked, while fresh. This compounds

across applications.

  • Note anything you fumbled and fix it before the next round.

8.7Red Flags To Watch For (Evaluate Them Too)#

You are assessing them as well. Watch for:

  • Vagueness about what you would actually work on
  • "We're like a family" plus questions about your willingness to work weekends
  • No clear answer on what the role's first 90 days look like
  • Interviewers who have not read your resume
  • Pressure to accept immediately, or an exploding offer deadline
  • Reluctance to put the offer in writing with full CTC breakdown
  • Very high attrition visible on LinkedIn (many people with 1-year tenures)
  • Evasiveness about travel or on-site expectations
  • A "Senior Consultant" title that on inspection means the same work at a

different label

QUESTIONS THAT SURFACE THESE

  • "What does a typical week look like for someone in this role?"
  • "Why is this role open - growth, or a replacement?"
  • "How long has the team been together?"
  • "What's the on-site/travel expectation?"

8.8The Things That Lose Offers (Unrelated To Skill)#

In rough order of how often they matter:

  1. RAMBLING. Answers over two minutes with no structure. This is the single

biggest risk for you specifically, because you have a lot to say. Rehearse to time.

  1. OVERCLAIMING AND BEING CAUGHT. One inflated claim contaminates everything

else you said. PART 1 SECTION 1.6 exists for this.

  1. BADMOUTHING. Accenture, T-Mobile, a colleague, a previous manager. Never.
  2. NO QUESTIONS FOR THEM. Reads as low interest.
  3. NOT KNOWING THE COMPANY. Unforgivable and entirely preventable.
  4. DEFENSIVENESS on the experience-level question. It is a fair question.

Answer it once, well, and move on.

  1. LEADING WITH MONEY. It is a legitimate motivation; it is not a good answer

to "why this role."

  1. CONFIDENTIALITY CARELESSNESS. Volunteering client detail you should not.

Ironically, restraint here IMPROVES your candidacy.

  1. ARRIVING EXHAUSTED. Do not cram the night before.
  2. NOT FOLLOWING UP. A short thank-you note is cheap and most people skip it.

8.9If You Don'T Get It#

Worth planning for, because it is a realistic outcome and how you handle it determines what the week was worth.

  • ASK FOR FEEDBACK. Politely, once. Most firms give little, but some give

something specific and useful.

  • The week's work is NOT wasted. The labs, stories, resume, and recorded

practice apply to every Power Platform senior role. You are better prepared for the next application than you were for this one.

  • If the feedback is "not enough experience," that is information about the

firm's bar, not a verdict on you. Apply to firms whose bar matches.

  • If the feedback is a specific skill gap, close it and reapply in six to

twelve months. Firms do rehire candidates who visibly closed a gap - it is a strong signal.

  • KEEP THE MOMENTUM. The worst outcome is doing this week's work, not getting

this role, and stopping. Apply to the next five.

8.10Honest Limitations Of This Document Set#

Stated plainly so you calibrate:

  1. I BUILT THIS FROM YOUR REPO AND OUR CONVERSATION. I have not seen your

resume, your actual employment dates, your degree, your current salary, or the company you are applying to. Several sections have placeholders.

  1. THE QUESTION BANK IS BROAD, NOT EXHAUSTIVE. I derived it from the JD and

from common Power Platform interview patterns. You will be asked things that are not in here. The point is that you will have answered enough adjacent questions to think clearly under pressure.

  1. THE COST/PROGRAM DETAILS IN PART 7 ARE FROM TRAINING KNOWLEDGE, NOT

VERIFIED TODAY. Microsoft changes program terms. Verify at signup. Every item has a documented fallback, so the plan survives a closed route.

  1. I HAVE NOT VERIFIED YOUR CLAIMS. Everything about your role, the team

structure, the stakeholder relationship, and the PAD experience came from you. If any of it is stated more strongly here than is true, FIX IT before you use it. My scoring assumed your account is accurate.

  1. SALARY GUIDANCE IS DIRECTIONAL. I do not have current Gurugram market data

for this role. Research it yourself - AmbitionBox, Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, and peers. Do not anchor on the figures I suggested without checking.

  1. ONE WEEK OF LABS IS ONE WEEK OF LABS. It converts "never touched" to

"hands-on at small scale." An interviewer who has run tenant governance for years WILL find the edge of what you know if they push. The plan's answer is honesty, which works with most good interviewers - but not with all.

  1. I CANNOT PREDICT THE OUTCOME. As I said before: this materially improves

your odds. It does not guarantee anything. Factors outside your control - their experience bar, other candidates, budget, chemistry - matter and are not addressable by preparation.

8.11The One-Page Summary (If You Read Nothing Else)#

YOUR PITCH

3.5 years. Sole Accenture resource embedded at T-Mobile for 9 months in an all-client team. Shaped requirements with the business owner, built Canvas + Dataverse + Power Automate, integrated REST into CCure 9000 physical access control, owned four-environment ALM including production revert runbooks, demoed and trained client staff. Built a 111-tool MCP server exposing Power Platform to AI assistants with three auth modes and secure-by-default gating, then rebuilt the architecture for UKG Dimensions.

YOUR THREE PILLARS

  1. I was the entire vendor presence.
  2. I build the tools that build the solution.
  3. I operate in production with the blast radius in mind.

YOUR THREE OBJECTIONS AND ONE-LINE ANSWERS

"Only 3.5 years" -> Answer with scope owned, not years served. "No tenant DLP" -> True; I was a maker not an admin; here's the model, and here's what I've since done in my own tenant. "One client" -> Broad surface; and I proved the method transfers by rebuilding it for a different platform.

WHAT YOU MUST NOT CLAIM

Tenant-scale DLP administration. Production CoE deployment. Unattended RPA fleet. PL-600. Dataflow-based production integration. Any client PII.

THE HIGHEST-VALUE PREPARATION

Rehearsing the 10 STAR stories out loud, on video, under two minutes each. More valuable than any lab. If you cut anything, cut labs, not rehearsal.

THE SINGLE BIGGEST RISK

Rambling, or overclaiming and getting caught. Both are entirely within your control.
